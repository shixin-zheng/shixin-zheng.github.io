Treats Sobolev gradient flows for the Gross–Pitaevskii ground state as Riemannian gradient descent on the sphere under a metric induced by a discrete elliptic operator, and analyzes their convergence through the condition number of the Riemannian Hessian at the ground state; bounds on the Rayleigh quotient of that Hessian give a single analysis covering the classical shifted-Laplacian and energy-adaptive metrics, and suggest three new ones. Among them is a Thomas–Fermi metric whose condition number is bounded uniformly in the interaction strength for the one-dimensional harmonic trap, and whose iteration count does not grow in the Thomas–Fermi regime. Three-dimensional tests with up to one billion unknowns, for both Riemannian gradient descent and Riemannian conjugate gradient, match the analysis: the Thomas–Fermi flow needs the fewest iterations, while the metrics inverted exactly by tensor-product solvers are fastest in wall-clock time on one GPU.
